This is a starting point for a conversation about your rental home. It is not an energy audit, an installer quote or a personal health assessment.

What we compare

We estimate the running costs of the heating, cooktop and individual hot-water systems you identify. Insulation is assessed through practical guidance. Lighting, refrigeration, ovens, cooling and other household loads are outside the numerical subtotal.

The postcode checks the Victorian pilot’s residential range; it does not identify your address, weather station or distribution tariff. Dwelling type informs practical advice, but does not assign a building energy rating.

How the arithmetic works

We compare the same low and high useful-energy demand before and after an appliance upgrade. Purchased energy depends on appliance efficiency. The ranges are scenarios, not statistical confidence intervals.

Purchased energy = useful energy demand ÷ efficiency

Running cost = purchased energy × unit price

Operational emissions = purchased energy × fuel emission factor

Electricity is measured in kWh. Gas prices are entered per MJ, using 1 kWh = 3.6 MJ. We calculate cost and emissions separately. Dollar savings are never multiplied by carbon intensity.

Our illustrative demand assumptions

These are deliberately visible project assumptions, not measured usage or government household forecasts. Real outcomes depend on your home, climate, habits and equipment.

Demand assumptions for each appliance category
CategoryLow to high scenario
Heating60–120 kWh of useful heat per heated m² per year.
Cooktop75–150 kWh of useful cooking heat per person per year.
Hot water35–55 litres per person per day, a 45°C temperature rise and a 15% system-loss allowance.
InsulationGuidance only. No energy or dollar savings are added.

For hot water, litres × temperature rise × 4.186 gives heat in kilojoules. Dividing by 3,600 converts to kWh; multiplying by 365 and 1.15 gives yearly demand including the illustrative loss allowance.

Efficiency and prices

CategoryCurrent / replacement assumptions
HeatingGas 75%; conventional electric 100%; reverse-cycle COP 3.5.
CookingGas 40%; conventional electric 74%; induction 90%.

COP describes useful heat delivered per unit of electricity: a COP of 3.5 represents 3.5 units of heat for one unit of electricity. Model choices within published ranges are still assumptions, not the seasonal ratings of your appliances.

The default prices are 30 cents/kWh electricity, 3 cents/MJ gas and 100 cents/day gas supply. These are illustrative flat prices, not a live tariff, regulated offer or Victorian average. Enter the prices on your bill for a more relevant scenario. Time-of-use pricing, tariff tiers, discounts, solar self-consumption and batteries are not modelled.

Gas supply and combined savings

Appliance subtotals exclude fixed charges. We show a separate gas supply saving only when every identified gas appliance is replaced, no other or uncertain gas use remains, and you select ending the gas connection. The annual amount is daily charge × 365 and is counted once. Fees for disconnection or electrical upgrades are not included.

Households with unknown appliances, shared or solar-boosted hot water need further assessment. An omitted category is not a zero-cost appliance. A home already using an efficient electric type receives no assumed replacement saving.

Emissions can go up as well as down

We use the 2025 National Greenhouse Accounts factors: 0.78 kg CO₂e/kWh for Victorian grid generation and 0.05153 kg CO₂e/MJ for direct gas combustion. Upstream fuel emissions, embodied emissions, on-site solar and future grid changes are excluded.

Under this boundary and these factors, some gas-to-electric changes can increase estimated operational emissions. The comparison keeps that result visible. Electrification alone does not guarantee an immediate reduction.

Installation, rebates and rental requirements

Indicative costs are separated from running costs. They are not quotes and no unconfirmed rebate is subtracted. Victorian Energy Upgrades providers need to confirm eligibility, product suitability and the actual discount.

Rental standards have specific commencement dates, triggers and exemptions. We link to Consumer Affairs Victoria but do not decide that a landlord has a legal obligation or set a compliance deadline. A professional should assess the home before fixed work is agreed.

Your privacy and AI drafting

Your answers and edited letter stay in browser memory and are cleared on reload. The local server processes answers without saving them to a database. Opening this page in a new tab preserves the original assessment; navigating away in the same tab may reset it.

When an API key is configured, generating a letter sends only the selected improvement topics to your configured AI provider to draft an opening and closing. Names, street addresses, postcode, tariffs and your edited letter are not sent. Calculated evidence and sources are inserted locally. For OpenAI, response storage is disabled in the request. Other providers may have different storage controls; the selected provider’s data-handling policies apply.

AI does not calculate your figures, send messages or choose improvements for you. If drafting is unavailable, a personalised fixed-text letter remains available. Review every draft before using it.
